What Ndola buys

A manufacturing town, not a mining one

Ndola's demand profile is different from the mining towns further west. The hazards that come up most are the ones you find in workshops and process plants: hand injuries on sharp-edged material, hot work, chemical handling, and noise on production lines that run continuously.

That shifts the buying pattern. Ndola sites tend to order consumables in steady volume rather than large one-off kit-outs — gloves and respirators month after month, with footwear and workwear on a replacement cycle.

The lines that move here

  • Hand protection — nitrile grip gloves for general handling (C03), cut level 5 liners for sheet metal and sharp scrap (C04), and double-palm welding gloves for hot work (P10)
  • Eye and face — wraparound and Euro-style safety glasses (C06, C07) and welding shields with the right lens shade (P11)
  • Leather protection — aprons and spats against sparks and spatter (P08, P09)
  • Workwear — 65/35 conti suits with colour options for department identification (P01)
  • Hearing — 27 dB ear muffs on noisy lines (P13)
  • Chemical — acid-resistant worksuits (P04) and Type 5/6 disposable coveralls (C02)

Common questions

Supplying Ndola

Do you deliver PPE to Ndola?

Yes. Ndola is served from our Kitwe office, so deliveries are short-haul and orders placed in the morning are usually confirmed the same day. Give us the delivery point when you request a quote and we will include it in the quotation.

Can you supply a Ndola plant on standing order?

Yes. Standing supply suits sites that consume consumables predictably — gloves, respirators, ear plugs. Tell us your monthly run rate and we will price accordingly.

What footwear standard should we specify?

For general industrial work, EN ISO 20345 S3 with a cleated outsole. For hot work near furnaces or molten material, add an HRO-rated outsole. Safety footwear is a compulsory standard in Zambia under ZS ISO 20345 — our guide to PPE and the law explains what that means for procurement.
